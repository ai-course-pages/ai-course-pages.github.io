What AI means in this course

This page fixes the definition and the words around it. Later items go into data, uses, and methods. You can open those items before this one feels finished.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the ability of computer programs and robots to mimic human intelligence.

This is the working definition this course uses; other sources word it differently.

People meet that ability in work, in education, and in everyday life. This course looks at it mainly as something in society: what it does in daily life, and what follows from using it.

Words this course keeps

  • Human intelligence is the set of human abilities named in the definition, such as recognizing something, using language, or solving a problem.
  • Data is the information an AI system is given or produces. Quality and selection are on L1.C.2.
  • Application is a place AI is used. The first overview in this course names prediction, robotics, computer vision, and generative AI.
  • Technique is a kind of method. The first overview names search, classification, and object recognition. See L1.C.5.
  • Algorithm is a specific procedure. The first overview names decision trees, regression, and supervised and unsupervised learning. See L1.C.6.
  • Machine learning is one family of methods for training a system from data. In this course it sits inside AI, as one family among others.

Examples in everyday life

  • A phone keyboard suggests the next word. It imitates part of language use.
  • A photo app groups pictures that seem to show the same person. It imitates recognizing people in images.
  • A map app suggests a route around a delay. It imitates planning a route from available information.
  • A voice assistant turns spoken words into written words. It imitates recognizing speech and language.

How the words fit together

AI contains machine learning as one family of approaches. An application uses one or more techniques. A technique is carried out by algorithms. All of it runs on data: information that is given to a system or produced by it.

For the park app, the application is answering “is the park busy this afternoon?” A technique could classify the park as busy or quiet. An algorithm carries out that procedure, and machine learning is one possible way to make such a system learn from examples. The examples and later information it uses are data. The application and technique are different levels of description.

What AI is not, in this course

A plain calculator following fixed steps chosen by a person is usually not what this course means by AI. A system that learned a rule from data may fit the course definition when that rule lets a program mimic a human ability. The boundary can depend on what a system does and how it was made, so this is a useful distinction rather than a test for every case.

Narrow tasks and general intelligence

Many systems called AI are designed for a limited task, such as recognizing speech or sorting images. This is often called narrow AI. General intelligence is an idea for a system able to handle a broad range of tasks in ways associated with human intelligence. The terms describe different scopes; this page makes no claim about when, or whether, general intelligence will be achieved.

Summary

  • In this course, AI means programs and robots that mimic human intelligence.
  • The course uses six labels: human intelligence, data, application, technique, algorithm, and machine learning.
  • AI includes machine learning; applications use techniques, which algorithms carry out.
  • Data is part of how an AI system runs and what it can produce.
  • Many AI systems focus on a limited task; general intelligence is a separate idea about broader ability.
