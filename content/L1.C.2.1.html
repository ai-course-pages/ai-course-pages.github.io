What counts as data

The group starts at L1.C.2. The definition of AI in this course is on L1.C.1.

Data is the information an AI system is given or produces. People meet that information as lists, photos, messages, sounds, counts, and the labels someone has already attached.

Two directions matter. Information given to the system comes from outside it: a person, a sensor, a file, a log of what happened. Information the system produces comes back out: a label, a count, a sentence, a score. Both are data. The afternoon answer "busy" is produced data. The counts and photos that led there are given data.

The park, as information

The neighborhood app answers "is the park busy this afternoon?" The data for that question can include:

  • Counts of people passing an entrance, hour by hour.
  • Photos from a bench camera.
  • Short public posts about the park.
  • Labels a person already wrote, such as "busy" or "quiet", on past afternoons.

The wish for a true answer is not data. The app's name is not data. The data is the information the system can actually be given, and the information it writes back.

A table is one ordinary way to hold that information: a row for each hour, a column for the count. A photo is another way. The form can change. What makes it data is that the system is given it, or produces it.

Why the word is worth keeping straight

Later pages ask whether this information fits the use, and which part of it is used. Those questions have nothing to grip until "data" means this material, and not the hope for a good answer.
