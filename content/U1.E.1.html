Effort and how many tokens it spends

Some models do hidden work before they write the reply you see. Effort is the setting that allows more or less of that hidden work. The model stays the same. A higher effort spends more reasoning tokens, so the same question costs more and usually takes longer.

This course practices three settings: low, medium, and high. A fourth name, xhigh (Extra High), is here so you can recognize it. Leave Extra High, and any step above it, until low, medium, and high already feel familiar. Those higher settings spend many tokens, including on an ordinary question.

If these words are new

The welcome for this section is U1.0. It assumes you can send a prompt and read a reply, that what you send, the hidden work, and the reply are counted apart, and that a setting does not swap the model. This is the first working page.

What the tokens are paying for

A reply has three piles of tokens.

  • Input. Your message, instructions, and any source text you attached.
  • Reasoning. Hidden work before the reply. Effort is the knob on this pile.
  • Output. The reply you read.

A long reply can come from low effort. A short reply can come from high effort. The setting changes the hidden work. It does not set the length of the answer.

Raising effort is a poor fix for a vague question. Clean the question first. High effort on a vague question buys a thorough treatment of the vagueness.

The four names

EffortWhat you are buyingIn this course
low A little hidden work. Fits one clear step when the wording is already clean. Use
medium Enough hidden work for a normal task: a few constraints, a table, a passage you want kept intact. Use
high A lot of hidden work. Fits several parts, a conflict in the instructions, or a messy source. Use
xhigh, Extra High More hidden work than high. Some products stop here. Some offer a further step, often called max. The bill grows quickly. Wait

Products use these names, or they use a number (a thinking budget) that you can line up with the same four steps. A product may also offer minimal or none, below low. This course does not drill those.

How the names line up

On Grok

For grok-4.7 and grok-4.6 the values are low, medium, high, and xhigh. Reasoning stays on. You choose how much. If you leave the field unset, the call runs at high. grok-4.5 has no xhigh. On the multi-agent model, the same field selects how many agents take part, which is a different meaning. Keep that model off this comparison.

For the work in this course, set low, medium, or high on purpose. Extra High is a later experiment, after those three are familiar.
